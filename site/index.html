It starts with what's already on the record

When you start a campaign, Headcount looks the bill up on Congress.gov or Open States and fills in what it finds:

  • Sponsors and cosponsors are marked Yes.
  • Members who voted on the bill itself (passage, concurrence, a committee's favorable report) are marked the way they voted. Procedural votes like motions to recommit are left out.
  • The committee that has the bill is set, with its members, chair and ranking member.
  • The bill brief gets the sponsors, status, committees and official summary.

It checks again every day, so new cosponsors and votes show up on their own. Anyone you've already talked to is left exactly as you marked them.

Then you work the undecided

Meeting requests

Open a member and choose Request a meeting. The request goes to the scheduler or the right aide, from the official staff list where the chamber publishes one. Headcount offers three times, drafts the email and opens it in Gmail, Outlook or your own email app.

No official list publishes staff email addresses, so Headcount writes them in the format each chamber uses and labels them. If your firm subscribes to LegiStorm, upload its staff export and the real addresses are used instead.

Who to see next

A short list of the members who would move your odds the most, with how movable each one is and when anyone last talked to them.

Notes and follow-ups

Log a call or meeting on the member, set the follow-up, and the whole team sees it within seconds.

A brief for the meeting

Sponsors, status, committees and the official summary, filled in from the bill. Add your position and talking points, then print it or save it as a PDF.

Also in Headcount

Monitor

Tell it a few words, like "broadband" or "flood insurance", and it checks Congress, the states and new federal rules every day, and tells you what matches.

Supporter pages

A public page for each campaign. Supporters type their address, see their own legislators, and send a letter or call. Letters show up next to that member in your count.

Who else is lobbying

Federal lobbying disclosures that mention your bill: the clients, the firms and what they reported.

Contacts

Agency staff, coalition partners and reporters, with notes the team shares. Email a group from your own Gmail or Outlook.

Reports

A client update, the full whip count, or a quarter's contacts by client and issue for your lobbying disclosure.

On your phone

Add it to your home screen. It keeps working on a weak signal in the Capitol and catches up when you're back online.

Where the information comes from

U.S. House

Every current member, with office phones and the staff listed in the House's own directory. Votes from the House Clerk.

U.S. Senate

All 100 senators and their offices, with votes from the Senate. The Senate publishes no staff directory, so you add the staff you know.

Florida Legislature

The Senate and House for the 2026–2028 term, from flsenate.gov and flhouse.gov, with legislative and district aides. Checked weekly.

Other states

Current members, bills, votes and committees from Open States.
